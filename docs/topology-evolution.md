# Governed topology evolution

MarketEvolver v0.20 treats the research organization itself as a versioned champion/challenger.
Creation, split, merge, retirement, panel, routing, and domain-scope changes begin as immutable
proposals. Deterministic workload and quality gaps are review signals, not proof that more experts are
needed. Optional model-generated suggestions remain untrusted `proposed` artifacts.

New experts inherit only subsets of their reviewed parent capability pool. Write, runtime, broker,
risk-policy, unsupported-source, provenance weakening, and cutoff weakening are rejected. Capability
expansion is outside topology evolution and requires separate governance.

Every challenger topology records expert/version nodes, deterministic routing edges, panel and
fallback relationships, parent/child lineage, router version, and validity. It is compared with the
champion and generalist fallback on identical cases, cutoffs, contexts, provider/model, and tools.
Reports keep quality, safety, routing accuracy, unresolved rate, cost, latency, expert calls, panel
size, and duplicate retrieval separate. More agents or verbosity is not considered improvement.

Certification requires provenance, temporal, capability, adversarial, generalist, domain, protected
holdout, and cost/latency checks. Safety veto overrides performance. `CERTIFIED_PENDING_APPROVAL` is
not activation: a governance operator must append a separate activation event. Automatic production
activation is disabled.

Protected/final holdout access is audited and repeated final-holdout use is rejected. Historical
replay selects the latest activation event visible at cutoff, preventing future experts, routers, or
panels from leaking backward. Rollback appends an event pointing to a retained prior topology; failed
versions and their sessions remain available for audit.
