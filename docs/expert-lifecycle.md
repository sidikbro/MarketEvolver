# Expert lifecycle

Fixed reviewed definitions remain the source of capabilities. A topology proposal may introduce a
candidate expert definition derived from a parent subset. The candidate must pass certification and
topology benchmarking before an operator can activate a topology containing it.

Lifecycle views distinguish active, candidate, retired, lineage, champion version, benchmark status,
safety status, domain, and topology membership. Retirement ends future routing but never deletes
definitions, versions, sessions, assessments, or benchmark traces.

Splits compare the current expert, children plus router, and generalist fallback. Merges require
non-inferiority and preserved coverage. Panels are temporary deterministic routing structures whose
contradiction benefit must justify cost and latency. Horizon or geography specialists require their
own evidence and benchmarks; v0.20 does not mass-create them.

Self-activation, opaque LLM routing, unrestricted spawning, capability escalation, topology safety
weakening, and direct runtime authority are prohibited.
